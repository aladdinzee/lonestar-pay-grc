# Lonestar Pay : GRC Program

A complete governance, risk, and compliance program built for a fictional Texas fintech, taking it from no formal security program to SOC 2 Type II readiness, 20 working documents, from scoping through executive reporting.

> **Note** : Lonestar Pay and PayCore Solutions in this projects are assumed as fictional. Any co-incident with any real companies are purely co-incidental and not intended to depict them.

- All data, and report summaries were created for this Simulation by Claude AI. This was a self-directed learning project completed with AI-assisted guidelines. All decisions and documents were reviewed and finalized by me.

## The Scenario

Lonestar Pay is an Austin-based Fintech (about 120 employees) that lets roughly 8,000 small businesses accept card payments. Larger customer began asking for SOC 2 report, and the CEO set a 12-month goa. The company had no policies, no risk register, and no one owning compliance.

## My Role

GRC analyst, responsible for the program end to end : scoping, writing policies, assessing risk, mapping control, testing audit readiness, reviewing vendors, governing AI systems, and reporting to leadership.

## Whats inside

I divided this project into 8 separate Phases to to take a divide and conquer approach for this learning project. The Phases are numbemered as below : 

1. Scoping : Created the scoping memo.
2. Governance : Created the required policies.
3. Risk Management : Assessed the risks.
4. Controls : Mapped Controls to NIST CSF, ISO 27001 & SOC 2.
5. Audit Readiness : Created an evidence collection plan.
6. Vendor Risk : Assessed Vendor risk.
7. AI governance : Assessed AI-related risk and created policies supporting it.
8. Executive Wrap-up : Created an executive summary and recommendations to achieve the goal.

## Key Results

- 8 Risks scored with documented methdology. 2 remain High after exisitng controls (support chatbot data leakage and payment processor dependency.)

- 10 Controls mapped to SOC 2, PCI-DSS, and NIST CSF, then tested in a mock audit : 1 Ready, 4 Partial, 5 Gap.

- 9 remediation items with owners and dates, showing SOC 2 Type II window cannot reliastically start before February 2027.

- Vendor review of the payment processor ended in "accept with conditions" including the finding of Lonestar's own control gaps weaken the vendor's protections.

- Two AI Systems goverened under AI Use Policy and assessed against NIST AI RMF.

## How the Pieces Connect

Risks in risk register drive the controls in matrix. The matrix is tested in gap assessment, and gaps become remediation items. Vendor conditions reuse the same remediation items, and AI risks feed back into the register. The executive summary pulls the results togethor for the leadership.

## Skills Demonstrated

- Risk Assessment : scoring, treatment, and acceptance authority
- Policy Writing : testable, owner-assigned statements.
- Control Mapping : SOC 2, PCI-DSS, NIST CSF 2.0
- Audit Readiness : Evidence planning, sampling logic, gap analysis
- Third Party risk : tiering, questionaires, SOC 2 Report Review
- AI Governance : NIST AI RMF Functions
- Stakeholder Communication : RACI and executive reporting

## What i learned

- A policy is only as good as its evidence. Auditors tests whats written, so every statement has to be specific enough to be sampled.
- Scoping is a decision you must defend, and excluding a system needs a proper explanation.
- Vendors and your own gaps are linked. Lonestar's missing controls weakned the processor's protection for it.
- Honest findings beat clean scorecards. The most useful part of the audit was the red marks.
- The analyst co-ordinates and verifies, while the systme owner own the controls and decision.

## Limiations

This project is a simulation. Dollar amounts, dates, and vendor report contents are illustrative genated by AI. Real program requires owner validation, communcation between different team members, meetings, legal review, and an independent auditor.
